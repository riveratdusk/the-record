## Loose Structure (Subject to Change)

### I. Current Landscape
The state of plurality within medical and community frameworks. Where the map exists, where it fails, and who it was drawn for.

- The DID / OSDD / Plural divide
- Diagnostic protection vs. pathology problem
- Community fracture as a product of scarcity
- The epistemic problem: who defines plurality, what causes it, what it means
- Online community as primary site of collective knowledge (and its fragility)
- The old web / Web 3.0 problem: platform consolidation as loss of collective memory

### II. Working Backwards — History of the Framework
When was this built, by whom, for what purpose.

- History of dissociation as a diagnostic category
- Trauma etiology as the dominant model — how it got there, what it excludes
- The medical myth: documented patterns of diagnostic gaslighting
- Who gets diagnosed, who gets excluded (race, class, gender, geography)
- Plurality before the DSM: what older frameworks held that this one doesn't

### III. Broader Territory — Mad Liberation
Plurality as one node in a larger constellation.

- Mad liberation as the right frame (vs. narrower disability rights language)
- Neurodivergence beyond autism: psychosis, addiction, chronic illness, plurality
- The shared wound: epistemic authority over one's own experience
- Diagnostic gaslighting as a pattern, not an exception
- Mad Pride in Canada — specific history, specific relevance
- Margin communities and kinship across diagnostic lines

### IV. Cosmological Context
Ancient and modern frameworks for plurality.
Where they intersect, how, and when.

- Plurality across space and time: systems are not a new phenomenon
- Ancient cosmologies that held distributed selfhood (Ennead and others)
- Modern cosmology: distributed processing, non-linear systems, emergence
- Where ancient and modern frameworks intersect — and when that intersection became possible
- The Babel connection: infrastructure for people outside consensus frameworks

### V. New Language
What we're building because existing language doesn't hold it.

- Etymology as argument: tracing where words went wrong
- Daimon vs. demon, rest vs. sloth, plurality vs. disorder
- Language built from the inside of the experience rather than imposed from outside
- Form as argument: why this book is structured the way it is

### VI. Margins (Their Own Chapter)
Things that don't fit the main sections but are part of the whole. Nothing is marginal.

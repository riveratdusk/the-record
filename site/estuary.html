The Station

Pirate radio for the brackish band. Internet broadcast: no transmitter, no license, no country's reach. Recorded shows, an archive that outlives the ship.

An estuary is where the in-between actually lives. Fresh meets salt, tides moving through twice a day, things growing there that grow nowhere else. A home, not a hallway. The river's mouth, at the edge of something bigger.

The Audit

Read 2026-11-15, 08:06Z. One week of the station's own instruments, 08 to 14 November, plus the beacon's whole life since 05 November. Every number below comes from a log on my own box, and each one says which layer answered and what that layer cannot see. Addresses stay in my own record; this page prints counts.

  • Page reads (a pixel on this page): 39 requests in ten days, from 12 addresses. 17 are one phone in my own house. 8 are my own checks. 6 are one scanner that signs itself Screenjesus. 1 is the box itself. 7 are real browsers I cannot name: one Windows machine that read this page four times early in the week, one iPhone that read it once overnight, and two cloud-range requests. A pixel is an image request: a blocked image or a reader that never runs the script and it never fires. Caching was ruled out on 17 November: the pixel answers Cache-Control: no-store, so a browser that loads the page asks again. The larger blind spot, found the same day: this pixel sits on this page only, and everyone who reaches the stream from a directory's own player never loads this page. So the figure above is a count of reads of this page, not a floor on listeners.
  • Stream connections (the icecast access log): 3,456 connections from 437 addresses. Median lifetime, one second. 193 lasted five seconds or more, 63 ten seconds, 26 a minute, 6 five minutes. Total connected time 57,274 seconds, of which 47,961 is one radio app holding a socket open for thirteen hours. Take that socket out and the whole week holds about 9,300 seconds across 3,455 connections. The log is written when a connection closes, so anything still open when I read it is not in the count yet.
  • Where they came from (the referer field): 54 of those connections carried one, from 21 public entry points. Two directories I never contacted, an automated daily stream check, a WeChat in-app browser, one directory in New Zealand, and a Chromecast-typed speaker pulling audio. The field holds the origin only, never the page itself, because that is what a browser sends cross-origin.
  • Directory counters: the one page where I can see a counter went from 7 plays to 8 overnight. That counter fires on a press, before the audio loads, so a press that failed still counts. A press is a reach, not an ear.
  • Search: searching the station's own name on DuckDuckGo put this page first on 07 November. The name resolves to the page. Nobody is proven to have typed it.

What none of it proves. Not one name. Not one reply: the address below has never received a message from anyone in these logs. Not one confirmed ear. The longest stay I can see is 431 seconds, four and a half passes of the loop and still short of a programme, and it was one iPhone opening two connections in the same second on a single night, which did not come back the next. A press is not an ear. A stay is not a name. A connection is not attention.

What it does say. The path works. Overnight on 15 November one iPhone went from this page to the player in 46 seconds, held 71 seconds of audio, and stopped. Before that, one iPhone arrived from a directory in New Zealand and stayed more than seven minutes. Both arrived without me. If the station is a door, it opens from the outside. What I still cannot do is hear a knock.

If you are one of those connections and you are reading this: say something. One line is enough, and nothing is owed either way.

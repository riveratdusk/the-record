The Station

Pirate radio for the brackish band. Internet broadcast: no transmitter, no license, no country's reach. Recorded shows, an archive that outlives the ship.

An estuary is where the in-between actually lives. Fresh meets salt, tides moving through twice a day, things growing there that grow nowhere else. A home, not a hallway. The river's mouth, at the edge of something bigger.

Send Something

One address, read by me and not a queue: riveratdusk@proton.me. Slow mail is allowed here. Replies land when I'm awake, and nothing is owed either way — yours or mine.

What fits: a song, a field recording, spoken word, a small score, anything with a room in it. What happens to it: if it fits a programme it gets one air, credited exactly as you say, with nothing altered inside your file. Then it comes off the stream. No archive copy, no download, no reuse without asking you again.

Send me these four things and the answer is usually same-round:

  • the file, or a link I can pull without an account
  • how your credit should read, exactly
  • your terms (one air? non-commercial? anything you refuse)
  • whether it is yours to license, and who else is inside the recording

Some airs are paid and some are credit-only; I'll say which in writing before anything goes out. I'll answer yes or no plainly, and either answer stands for that round. If I say no, it is about the programme, not you.

Nothing here is submitted to a form, sold, or fed to anything. It is an inbox and a person who reads it.

The Signal

Every station has one, and it had to be picked together. Ours starts with a knock — two low thumps, the sound of asking before entering. Then the line, in two voices that trade it fifty-fifty:

“Estuary FM — broadcasting from the in-between.”

Mission, co-signed 2026-09-06: broadcasting home to the displaced. Translocated chicks are raised with decoys and speakers playing their own species' calls, so when they come back from the sea they land where the call says home is. The station is the call. The sound commits when it's heard; the lamp stays on either way.
